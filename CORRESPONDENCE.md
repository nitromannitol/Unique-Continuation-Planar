# Correspondence between the paper and the formalization

`paper/ucplanar.tex` is the pinned source of *Unique continuation on planar
graphs*. A registered theorem has one declaration in its own file under
`UCPlanar/Frozen/`, with a paper line anchor and label.

The bytes strictly between `FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and
`FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`, with one leading newline dropped and the trailing
newline retained, are hashed by `tools/freeze.py` and checked by
`tools/check_manifest.py`. Definitions shared by statements live in
`UCPlanar/Basic.lean` or `UCPlanar/Support/`.

## Transcription conventions

- Graph metric balls use `LatticeProb.Graph.closedBall`, defined with
  `SimpleGraph.edist`.
- Conductances and harmonicity use `LatticeProb.Network.IsCond` and
  `LatticeProb.Network.HarmonicOn`. The paper's Laplacian is the negative of
  `LatticeProb.Network.netLaplacian`; their zero equations agree.
- A cited result is a proposition under `UCPlanar/External/`, carried as an
  explicit hypothesis at a use site satisfying its source hypotheses.
- Numerical constants precede the radius, function, and density parameters on
  which they do not depend. Finite cardinalities are used only for finite sets.

The classical Liouville theorem for the periodic network, recalled at
ucplanar.tex:159, is proved rather than cited: a periodic network is recurrent
by the Nash-Williams criterion applied to the boundary cuts of the coordinate
boxes, whose total conductance grows at most linearly in the radius, and on a
recurrent network every bounded harmonic function is constant
(`UCPlanar/Support/LiouCHeat.lean`, `UCPlanar/Support/LiouKilled.lean`,
`UCPlanar/Support/LiouPeriodic.lean`).

The discrete Remez inequality of Buhovsky, Logunov, Malinnikova and Sodin,
Corollary 2.2, cited at ucplanar.tex:504, is proved rather than cited: between
consecutive points of the dense set at least `|S| - m` unit intervals contain
no root of the derivative, so the set where the polynomial is bounded has
positive Lebesgue measure, and the sharp Remez inequality — proved by Bojanov's
compression argument against the Chebyshev polynomial — carries the bound out
to twice the interval (`UCPlanar/Support/RemezCheb.lean`,
`UCPlanar/Support/RemezLagrange.lean`, `UCPlanar/Support/RemezMeasure.lean`,
`UCPlanar/Support/RemezDiscrete.lean`).

`PeriodicGraph` records connectedness, local finiteness, a proper coordinate
realization, a rank-two translation action and a finite set of vertex orbits.
`PeriodicPlaneGraph` adds noncrossing embedded edge paths, translation
invariance of the drawing, properness of its edges, a uniform bound on face
sizes and a uniform bound on the diameter of a face. Faces are connected components of the complement of the drawing.
Cycle interiors are bounded components of the plane complement of the cycle.

The topological coefficient is uniform in the degree and face-size bounds.
The upper-bound minimum radius precedes the ellipticity ratio, and the other
upper-bound constants precede the conductance scale and the function.
Polynomial degree means total degree at most the specified natural number.
The counterexample includes the normalization at the origin and exact diagonal
support of its harmonic function.

In N-006, the density threshold depends only on the periodic graph.  The outer radius and
the constants in its estimate may also depend on the periodic conductance.

The topological support uses the shared bounded-degree graph-ball estimate to
pack the short face paths and count fixed-width neighborhoods of boundary
vertices. Component representatives identify connectivity within each sign set.
The finite label count bounds the number of contacts by twice the number of
boundary labels under the alternating-connection exclusion. Walks avoiding a
cycle stay in one complementary plane component; simple paths admit a terminal
subpath meeting the boundary only at its initial vertex.

`PlanarBoundary` proves that a simple path with one boundary contact and a
strictly interior endpoint has its entire trace in the closed cycle domain.
`PlanarAttachments` supplies short interior attachments to both signs and
interior access from sign vertices to the boundary. `PlanarDomain` restricts
edges to that domain and preserves the component-boundary condition.
`PlanarOrder` orders the contacts around the cycle and counts their labels.
`Support.topological_of_crosscuts` combines these results into the uniform
density bound, with alternating-crosscut intersection as an explicit hypothesis.

The general Jordan curve and crosscut theorems are imported from the pinned
[Schoenflies library](https://github.com/alonamaloh/schoenflies-lean).
The passage from embedded graph cycles to its Jordan-curve interface remains
part of the topological node.
