# Unique-Continuation-Planar

A Lean 4 formalization of
> **Unique continuation on planar graphs**  
> Ahmed Bou-Rabee, William Cooperman, Shirshendu Ganguly, [Discrete Analysis 2025:16](https://doi.org/10.19086/da.144015)

This repository formalizes the eight statements of the paper registered in `ledger/manifest.yaml`: the quantitative Liouville theorem, the exponential upper bound, the zero case, the topological lemma, the polynomial approximation lemma, the three-ball proposition, the exponential lower bound and the non-planar counterexample. It rests on Mathlib, on the shared library [Lattice-Probability](https://github.com/nitromannitol/Lattice-Probability) (`LatticeProb`), which supplies graph balls, conductances and harmonicity, and on the pinned [Schoenflies library](https://github.com/alonamaloh/schoenflies-lean), which supplies the planar Jordan and crosscut theorems. Three results that the paper cites from the literature are stated as explicit hypotheses.

## The model

A periodic planar graph `G = (V, E)` is drawn in the plane so that the drawing is invariant under translation by a rank-two lattice `𝓛`, with finitely many vertex orbits. Each edge carries a positive conductance `a`, and the Laplacian is `Δf(u) = Σ_{w∼u} a(u,w)(f(u) − f(w))`. The classical Liouville theorem says that a bounded harmonic function on `G` is constant. The main result, `theorem:liouville`, asks for the bound only on most of the graph: if `a` is `𝓛`-invariant, there is `ε = ε(a) > 0` such that every `f` with `Δf = 0` on `G` and

    lim_{n→∞} |{|f| ≤ 1} ∩ B_n| / |B_n|  ≥  1 − ε

is constant, where `B_n` is the graph-metric ball of radius `n` about a fixed origin. The proof sets an exponential upper bound (`theorem:uniformly-bounded`), which rests on a topological property of the level sets of planar harmonic functions, against an exponential lower bound (`theorem:lower-bound`). The paper also shows that planarity is needed: `theorem:counterexample` gives non-planar conductances on `ℤ²` with a harmonic function supported on a diagonal.

## Terms

These words are used throughout with fixed meanings.

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **registered statement**, also called a **node** | one Lean declaration transcribing one statement of the paper, or one result the paper cites. Each has an entry in `ledger/manifest.yaml`. |
| **frozen statement** | the declaration's text between the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`, pinned by the SHA-256 of those bytes. It cannot change without the change being recorded in the manifest. The proof after the end marker may be rewritten freely. |
| **`SEALED`** | the node is **proved** here: a complete proof, whose axiom closure contains only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. |
| **`FROZEN`** | the node is **assumed**, not proved: a result the paper quotes from the literature, stated as a proposition in `UCPlanar/External/` and carried as an explicit hypothesis by every theorem whose proof uses it. Every one is listed with its statement in [`ASSUMPTIONS.md`](ASSUMPTIONS.md). |
| **`DRAFT_SORRY`** | the statement is pinned but its proof is still open, registered as a `sorry`. |
| **cited input**, **external** | a `FROZEN` node, in the sense above. |
| **axiom closure** | the axioms a proof ultimately rests on, as reported by Lean's `#print axioms`. |
| **`sorryAx`** | the axiom Lean inserts for an unproved `sorry`; its presence in an axiom closure means the result is not proved. |

The word *frozen* carries two senses, so it is worth separating them: every
registered statement is frozen in the sense of being **pinned by hash**, while
the state `FROZEN` marks the subset that is **assumed rather than proved**.

## How a statement is tied to the paper

Each registered statement is a single declaration in its own Lean file, placed between the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`. `ledger/manifest.yaml` records, for each node, the SHA-256 of the bytes between the markers (one leading newline dropped, the trailing newline kept) as `frozen_sha256`, together with a `source:` entry giving the line range in `paper/ucplanar.tex` and, for the paper's numbered statements, the LaTeX label the declaration transcribes, for example `ucplanar.tex:161-167 (theorem:liouville)`. The three cited inputs carry a line range and a description of the cited result in place of a label. `tools/check_manifest.py` recomputes the hashes, so a statement cannot change without the manifest changing. For a theorem, the proof follows the end marker and may be rewritten freely.

## Building and checking

Lean and Mathlib are pinned by `lean-toolchain` and `lake-manifest.json`. `Lattice-Probability` and the Schoenflies library are git dependencies pinned to commits in `lakefile.lean` and `lake-manifest.json`.

```sh
elan toolchain install $(cat lean-toolchain)
lake exe cache get      # optional: prebuilt Mathlib
lake build UCPlanar
```

| `python3 tools/check_constants.py` | In each frozen statement whose conclusion has a real existential constant, no radius, function, density or conductance parameter is bound before it, so the constant does not depend on them. |
| `python3 tools/check_warnings.py` | Runs `lake build UCPlanar` and fails if the build fails or emits any warning other than the Lake notice about the Mathlib URL and the `sorry` warnings expected for `DRAFT_SORRY` nodes (none is registered). |

## Scope

Formalized: the eight registered statements `theorem:liouville`, `theorem:uniformly-bounded`, `theorem:zero-case`, `lemma:topological-lemma`, `theorem:counterexample`, `lemma:poly-approx`, `prop:three-ball` and `theorem:lower-bound`, on a periodic plane graph defined in `UCPlanar/Support/`. Such a graph is connected and locally finite, with a proper coordinate realization, a rank-two translation action and finitely many vertex orbits. Its drawing has noncrossing embedded edge paths, is translation invariant, and has uniform bounds on the size and the diameter of a face.

Assumed as hypotheses, not proved here: Janiszewski's theorem at a point for the drawings of two finite edge sets (`EdgeSplitting`), unicoherence of the sphere in the form that the frontier of a bounded face of a connected plane graph is connected (`Unicoherence`), and the discrete Moser estimate (`MoserEstimate`). The classical Liouville theorem for the periodic network and the discrete Remez inequality, both cited in the paper, are proved here.

Where the formalized statements or proofs differ from the published text, `PROOF.md` gives the reason. In summary:

- The reduction in Step 1 of the zero case, which deletes and contracts finite edge-connected components, is not formalized. The statement is proved directly for the periodic plane graph, with the surrounding cycles supplied by the cited plane inputs.
- The paper states the exponential lower bound on the unit square lattice and proves the periodic form in the appendix. The Lean statement is the periodic form.
- The three-ball proposition fixes the outer radius at `4N` in the paper. The Lean statement allows any outer radius `k ≥ 4`, chosen after the conductance.
- The topological lemma takes a degree bound and a face-size bound, and its constant depends on both. The paper states dependence on the maximum face size.
- The Lean statement of `theorem:liouville` makes the limit explicit: the density of `{|f| ≤ 1}` in `B_n` converges to a real number `d` with `1 − ε ≤ d`.
- The counterexample is stated in the sharper form that the paper's proof gives: the harmonic function is normalized at the origin and is nonzero exactly on the diagonal. It concerns one family of conductances on the square lattice with crossing edges and says nothing about other graphs.

Not formalized: the introduction, the related work and the applications the paper discusses, the remark following `theorem:zero-case`, and the figures. The `paper/` directory contains only `ucplanar.tex`.
